Sine and cosine

Two shadows of an arrow turning around a circle.

01 Meaning

Draw a circle of radius 1, the unit circle, and an arrow from its centre to a point on the rim. Turn the arrow through an angle θ from the rightward direction. Shine a light from above and from the side: the arrow's shadows on the axes are what we study.

The horizontal shadow is the cosine, cos θ. The vertical shadow is the sine, sin θ. Angles are measured in degrees (a full turn is 360°) or radians (a full turn is 2π).

02 Picture

Turn the arrow with the slider. Blue is the cosine, teal dots the sine. Watch them change sign as the arrow crosses an axis.

Turn around the unit circle

03 Real life

  • The height of a seat on a Ferris wheel as it turns.
  • A sled pulled by a rope at an angle: only the horizontal part pulls it forward.
  • A pure musical tone: the air pressure at one spot rises and falls like a sine over time.
  • A small swinging pendulum: its sideways position is approximately a sine over time (the approximation fails for big swings).

Recognition rule. If you need the part of a length or force that lies along a direction, think cosine; the part across it, think sine.

Spot it. A sled is pulled with a 50 N rope at 60° above the ground. How much of that force pulls it forward?

04 Worked example

Predict, then reveal

Cosine is the horizontal position on the unit circle. Where is the arrow's tip horizontally?

05 Why and when

Cosine and sine split a slanted quantity into the part along an axis and the part across it, and they describe anything that cycles. Without them, tilted arrows and repeating waves have no simple handle.

They suit rotation and oscillation; steady growth or decay needs exponents instead.

06 How to arrive at it

● Motivated ◆ Convention

In a right triangle, “adjacent over hypotenuse” gives cosine for angles up to 90°. Defining it on the unit circle is Motivated: it extends the same idea to every angle, including those past 90°, where the old triangle picture breaks. The unit of angle is a Convention: 360 degrees is a historical choice, while radians are the unit that makes later formulas simplest.

07 In an LLM

Cosine is how we compare vectors: cosine similarity of two vectors is the cosine of the angle between them, built from the dot product (Module 2).

Sine and cosine also appear directly. The original Transformer added fixed positional encodings made of sines and cosines to its token vectors, so the model can tell positions apart; for position 1, the first pair (the highest frequency, with a wavelength of 2π) is (sin 1, cos 1) = (…, …) (Vaswani et al., 2017). The paper also tried learned position vectors and reported nearly identical results.

Simplified. The full encoding uses many frequencies across the vector (with d_model = 512 in the base model). Many later models use other ways of encoding position.

Teach it

Everyday analogy

The shadow of a stick held at a slant: from above you see its horizontal part, from the side its vertical part. Where it breaks down: a real shadow has no sign, but cosine and sine can be negative.

Common misconception

“Sine and cosine are only for triangles.” They describe the position on a circle at any angle, and triangles are just the first case.
