Logarithms

A logarithm counts how many times you multiplied.

01 Meaning

Start at 1 and keep multiplying by 2: 1, 2, 4, 8. How many multiplications does it take to reach 8? Three. That count, three, is the logarithm of 8 for the base 2. The base is the number you keep multiplying by.

We write it log₂ 8 = 3. In general, log_b x = k means “multiply 1 by b exactly k times to get x”, which is the same as bᵏ = x.

The count does not have to be whole. 50 sits between 10 and 100, so its base-10 logarithm sits between 1 and 2 (about …).

The useful consequence: gluing two runs of multiplications end to end adds their counts. So the logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms.

log(ab) = log a + log b

03 Real life

  • Earthquake magnitude. Each whole step up the scale means the ground moves about … times as far (amplitude on a seismograph), but releases roughly 30 times as much energy (about … under the classic 1.5 relation; USGS also gives a newer estimate near 27), not 10. Two steps: … times the amplitude and about … times the energy under the classic relation.
  • Decibels. A rise of 10 dB is … times the power. A rise of 20 dB is … times the amplitude (of pressure or voltage), because power goes as amplitude squared, which makes 20 dB also … times the power. Power doubling is about … dB.
  • pH. Each step down is roughly ten times the effective concentration of hydrogen ions (strictly, their activity). Pure water at room temperature sits near pH ….
  • Octaves. Each octave doubles the frequency. From a note at 110 Hz to one at 880 Hz is … octaves.
  • Bits. Picking one of 8 equally likely options takes … yes-or-no questions.

Recognition rule. If equal steps on a scale mean equal multiplications, not equal additions, the scale is logarithmic.

A caution: “human perception is logarithmic” is only a rough rule. Loudness and pitch follow ratios fairly well over a middle range, but not exactly, and other fits, such as power laws, describe some senses better. Treat it as a reason log scales are handy, not a law.

Spot it. A news report says a magnitude 7 earthquake struck, after a magnitude 6 earlier in the week. Compared with the magnitude 6, how much larger is the ground motion (amplitude) of the magnitude 7?

05 Why and when

Numbers made by repeated multiplication span enormous ranges: the quietest and loudest sounds people can comfortably hear span about 120–130 dB SPL (decibels of sound pressure level, relative to 20 µPa). At 120 dB that is a power ratio of about …. Logarithms squeeze that range into small, evenly spaced numbers and turn multiplying into adding, which is far easier to handle.

They fit quantities that grow or shrink by ratios. They do not fit zero or negative numbers (no number of multiplications gives them), and they are the wrong tool when changes are additive.

06 How to arrive at it

✓ Derived ◆ Convention

  1. The requirement. We want a number that turns multiplying into adding: f(a × b) = f(a) + f(b).
  2. Obvious candidate. Use the number itself, f(x) = x.
  3. Why it fails. 3 × 4 = 12, but 3 + 4 = 7, not 12.

Predict: what could you count about a product that adds up when you multiply?

I have made my prediction: show the reasoning

Count the multiplications by the base. Multiplying b^j by b^k lays j runs and k runs end to end, giving b^(j+k), so the counts add. The idea is forced by the requirement (Derived). The choice of base is a Convention: another base only multiplies every logarithm by the same constant, which is why base 2 (bits), base 10 and base e (the natural logarithm) can be used interchangeably up to units.

07 In an LLM

A language model assigns a probability to each next token, and the probability of a whole sequence is the product of the individual probabilities (Module 5). Multiply 400 probabilities of 0.01 and the exact answer is a 1 with 800 zeros after the decimal point; ordinary double-precision arithmetic has nothing that small and returns …. Add the natural logs instead and the total is a comfortable …. This is why models and their training code work with log-probabilities.

Logs also set the units of the usual quality measures. If a model spreads its probability evenly over 4 tokens, the surprise at each is ln 4 = … nats, or … bits. The perplexity is e raised to the average surprise in nats, which comes to …: as unsure as a choice among four equally likely tokens.

Simplified. Real per-token probabilities vary; 0.01 and a uniform spread over four tokens are chosen so the arithmetic is visible. Perplexity is defined as the exponential of the average negative log-probability; using bits and 2 raised to the power gives the same number.

Explain it back

A logarithm counts how many times you multiply by the base to reach a number. Multiplying two numbers joins their runs of multiplications end to end, so the counts add. That is why log(ab) = log a + log b, and why logarithms turn long products into short sums.

Teach it

60-second explanation

“Start at 1 and keep multiplying by 10. 10, 100, 1,000. The logarithm just counts the steps: three. It turns a huge range into small evenly spaced numbers, which is why earthquakes, sound and acidity are reported on log scales. And since gluing two runs of tens together adds the counts, multiplying numbers becomes adding their logs.”

Everyday analogy

Counting the zeros in a number: 1,000 has three, 1,000,000 has six. Where it breaks down: it works only for exact powers of ten; real logarithms also handle numbers like 50.

Whiteboard sequence

  1. Draw a row of boxes 1, 10, 100, 1,000 with ×10 arrows.
  2. Label the arrows 1, 2, 3 as you count them.
  3. Circle 1,000 and write log₁₀ 1000 = 3.
  4. Draw a second row of equal spacing, one box per power.
  5. Show 100 × 1,000 as two runs joined, 2 + 3 = 5.

Questions learners ask

Is a logarithm of 1 always 0? Yes: zero multiplications leave you at 1.

Why is there no log of 0? No amount of multiplying 1 by a positive number ever lands on 0.

Why does an earthquake step differ for amplitude and energy? They are different quantities. Measured amplitude is defined to grow tenfold per step, while an empirical relation between magnitude and energy says energy grows by roughly 30 times per step (101.5 ≈ 32 under the classic relation; USGS also gives a newer estimate near 27). Both are multiplications; the sizes differ.

Common misconception

“A 20 dB sound is twice as powerful as a 10 dB sound.” It is 10 times the power. Equal steps on a log scale are equal multiplications, not equal additions.
