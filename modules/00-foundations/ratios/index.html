Ratios, percentages and averages

Compare by dividing; summarise by averaging.

01 Meaning

A ratio compares two amounts by dividing: 3 cups of flour to 1 cup of water is a ratio of 3 to 1. A percentage is a ratio written out of 100: …% means 15 out of 60.

An average squeezes a list into one number. The mean shares the total out evenly; the median is the middle value of the sorted list. They answer different questions.

02 Picture

Four values stay fixed. Drag the fifth outward: the mean ▲ chases it, while the median ■ stays put.

Mean versus median

03 Real life

  • A recipe scaled up keeps the same ratio of ingredients.
  • A 20% discount: multiply the price by 0.8.
  • Average house prices in a town: one very expensive sale drags the mean, the median hardly moves.

Recognition rule. If a number only makes sense relative to something else, it is a ratio. If one huge value is distorting a summary, check the median.

Spot it. A savings rate goes from 2% to 3%. How should it be described?

04 Worked example

Predict, then reveal

Add the three values, then divide by how many there are.

05 Why and when

Ratios and percentages let you compare things of different sizes: 30 out of 40 and 60 out of 100 can be put side by side. Averages let you describe a pile of numbers with one. Without them, comparing needs the whole raw list.

Use the mean for fairly symmetric data and when the total matters; use the median when a few extreme values would mislead. An outlier is such a value.

06 How to arrive at it

◆ Convention ● Motivated

“Out of 100” is a Convention: we could have used out of 10 or 1,000. Choosing the mean or the median is Motivated by the question: the mean when you care about the total, the median when you care about the typical case. There is no single correct average.

07 In an LLM

A training or validation loss is a mean: the per-token loss averaged over the tokens (and texts) in a batch. Benchmark scores are usually percentages: correct answers as a share of all questions. The link is direct, but the details of which tokens are averaged and how vary between training setups.

Teach it

Everyday analogy

The mean is where a see-saw balances; the median is the person in the middle of a queue. Where it breaks down: a queue has no weights, so a very tall person at the end doesn't tip anything.

Common misconception

“Up from 2% to 3% is a 1% rise.” It is one percentage point, which is a 50% rise relative to the old rate.
