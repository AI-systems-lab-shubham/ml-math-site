Slope and linear functions

A straight line changes by the same amount for every equal step across.

01 Meaning

Walk up a ramp. For every step you take forward, you rise by the same amount. How steep the ramp is, how much you go up per step forward, is its slope: a steady rate of change. Where the ramp starts, its height at the very beginning, is the intercept.

A linear function packs both facts into one rule:

y = mx + b

Here m is the slope (rise per step across), b is the intercept (the height at x = 0), x is the step count and y the height.

02 Picture

Change the slope and the line tilts; change the intercept and it slides up or down. The small burgundy triangle shows “run 1, rise m” at the start. The ochre dot is the intercept.

Draw the line y = m·x + b

03 Real life

  • A tank that starts with some water and fills at a steady number of litres per minute.
  • A cyclist at constant speed who starts some distance down the road.
  • A taxi fare: a fixed pick-up fee plus a steady price per kilometre.
  • A road gradient: metres climbed for each hundred metres travelled.

Recognition rule. If the same step in one quantity always changes the other by the same amount, it is linear. The slope is that amount; the intercept is where you start.

Spot it. A candle is 20 cm tall and burns down 2 cm every hour. Which rule gives its height h after t hours?

04 Worked example

Predict, then reveal

How far up does it go, and how far across? Divide the first by the second.

05 Why and when

A straight line is the simplest relationship that still has a direction and a size. It is the baseline we compare other shapes to. Zoom in far enough on almost any smooth curve and it looks like a line, which is the idea behind derivatives (Module 4).

It does not fit processes where the step depends on the current size, or where an effect levels off.

06 How to arrive at it

✓ Derived

  1. The requirement. Each equal step across changes the height by the same amount, m.
  2. Start. At x = 0 the height is b.
  3. Take steps. After 1 step: b + m. After 2 steps: b + m + m.

Predict: what is the height after x steps?

I have made my prediction: show the reasoning

After x steps you have added m exactly x times to b, so the height is b + x copies of m, which is mx + b. Nothing else is possible if every step adds the same amount, so the formula is forced: it is Derived.

07 In an LLM

A single artificial neuron computes a weighted sum plus a bias. With one input it is exactly this line:

z = w x + b

The weight w plays the role of the slope and the bias b plays the role of the intercept. With many inputs the same pattern becomes a sum of weight times input over all the inputs, plus the bias. Transformer layers are built from such weighted sums (Vaswani et al., 2017).

Simplified. Most units apply an activation function after this step, which bends the output. We meet that in Module 10. “Neuron” is a metaphor; the unit is just this formula.

Explain it back

m is how much the output rises for each one-step increase in the input: the steepness and direction. b is where the line starts, the output when the input is zero.

Teach it

60-second explanation

“Imagine a ramp. How far up it starts is b. How steep it is is m: each step forward lifts you by m. After x steps forward you have gone up x times m, plus the b you started with. That is y = mx + b. A neuron in an AI is the same recipe with weights and a bias.”

Everyday analogy

A taxi fare: pick-up fee plus a price per kilometre. Where it breaks down: real fares often have minimums or caps, which bend the line.

Whiteboard sequence

  1. Draw axes and mark a dot at height b on the vertical axis.
  2. Draw one step across and one rise of m.
  3. Repeat the step three times.
  4. Join the points into a line.
  5. Write y = mx + b and label each part.

Questions learners ask

What if the slope is negative? The line goes down as you move right.

What if the slope is zero? A flat line: the output never changes.

Why is it called linear? Because the graph is a line.

Common misconception

“Slope is the angle.” Slope is rise over run, a ratio. Doubling the slope does not double the angle.
