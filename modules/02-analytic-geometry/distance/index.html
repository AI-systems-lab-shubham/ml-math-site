Distance

How far apart two points are: subtract one from the other, then measure the difference with a norm.

01 Meaning

To find how far apart two places are, you first find the arrow that goes from one to the other. That arrow is the difference: subtract the start point from the end point entry by entry. Then you measure the arrow with a norm, as on the Norms card. That is the distance: the norm of the difference.

From A = (1, 2) to B = (4, 6) the difference is (3, 4). Its L2 length is …, the straight-line distance. Its L1 length is …, the walk along the grid. Swap A and B and the difference flips sign, but the norm does not change, so distance has no direction.

d(a, b) = ‖a − b‖

02 Picture

The two dots are fixed. Choose how to travel from A to B and compare the two lengths.

Distance is the length of a difference

03 Real life

  • Two phones on a map: the straight-line gap is an L2 distance of their positions.
  • Two students’ marks in the same four tests: subtract test by test and measure the gap between the two lists.
  • Two colours as (red, green, blue) amounts: the size of the difference says how different they look, roughly.

Recognition rule. If you subtract two lists of the same length and then measure the result, you are computing a distance.

Spot it. A music app wants to find songs “close” to the one you are playing. Each song is described by a list of numbers. What can it do with two such lists to say how close the songs are?

04 Worked example

Predict, then reveal

Subtract entry by entry: 6 − 2 and 2 − 5. Drop the signs and add.

05 Why and when

Distance turns “how similar are these two things?” into one number, as long as each thing is a list of numbers. It is how a program decides which examples are neighbours.

When it does not fit. If one entry is measured in huge units (millimetres) and another in small ones (years), the big units swamp the sum. Put entries on comparable scales first.

06 How to arrive at it

● Motivated

  1. The requirement. A distance should be zero when the two points are the same, never negative, and the same from A to B as from B to A.
  2. The candidate. The difference is zero exactly when the points match, so measuring it with a norm gives all three properties: the norm of 0 is 0, a norm is never negative, and flipping the arrow does not change its length.
  3. Why it is a good fit. Other distances exist, but this one lets any norm from the previous card become a distance, so we pick the norm that suits the problem.

Before you continue, predict: if you move both points by the same amount, does the distance change?

I have made my prediction: show the reasoning

No. Both points shift by the same vector, so the difference is unchanged, and so is its norm.

Teach it

Everyday analogy

Two pins on a map and a string between them: the string’s length is the distance. Where it breaks down: the string is always the straight line, but a norm can also measure a grid walk.

Common misconception

“Distance is the difference.” The difference is an arrow with a direction and can be negative; the distance is its length and never is.
