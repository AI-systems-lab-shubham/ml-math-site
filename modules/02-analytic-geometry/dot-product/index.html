Dot product

One number that says how much two arrows lean the same way: a shadow, a sum of products, and a cosine, all at once.

01 Meaning

Shine a lamp straight down on arrow a so its shadow falls on the line along arrow b. The shadow has a length, and the dot product is that shadow length multiplied by the length of b. If a leans the same way as b, the shadow points forwards along b and the number is positive. If a is at right angles to b it casts no shadow and the number is zero. If a leans against b the shadow points backwards and the number is negative.

To compute it, multiply the matching entries and add the products. For a = (3, 2) and b = (4, 0) that is 3×4 + 2×0 = …. The same number comes from the lengths and the angle: ‖a‖‖b‖ cos θ, where θ is the angle between the arrows (the Sine and cosine card explains cosine). The two formulas always give the same number (the site’s tests check this on hundreds of random pairs).

a·b = Σ aᵢbᵢ = ‖a‖‖b‖ cos θ

03 Real life

  • Work. Work is force · displacement. A sled pulled by a rope with a force of 20 N at 30° to the ground over 5 m: 20 × 5 × cos 30° ≈ … J. If the pull were at right angles to the motion it would do … J of work.
  • A shadow. The shadow of a stick on the ground, when the sun is overhead, is the stick’s part along the ground.
  • Light on a tilted surface. A solar panel tilted away from the sun catches less light: how much follows the cosine of the tilt.
  • A shopping bill. Multiply each price by its quantity and add: that is the dot product of the price list and the quantity list.

Recognition rule. If you multiply matching items and add them up, or you ask how much of one push goes along another direction, think dot product.

Spot it. A rope pulls a sled, but the rope is at exactly right angles to the direction the sled moves. How much work does the pull do on the sled in that direction?

Each entry of a matrix-vector product is one dot product, of a row with the vector, as on the Matrix-vector and matrix-matrix multiplication card.

04 Worked example

Predict, then reveal

Multiply the first entries, multiply the second entries, then add. Mind the minus sign.

05 Why and when

The dot product turns two lists into one number that says how much they agree. It also gives a length: a·a is the squared length of a, so every norm on the earlier card hides a dot product. It is also the building block of matrix multiplication, which is a table of dot products.

One thing to watch. The dot product grows with the lengths of the arrows as well as with how well they line up, so a long arrow gives a big number even if it is only slightly aligned.

06 How to arrive at it

✓ Derived

  1. The requirement. We want a number f(a, b) that is the same whichever arrow comes first, scales when either arrow scales and adds when either arrow adds, and that gives the squared length when both arrows are the same: f(a, a) = ‖a‖².
  2. Use the lengths we already have. By Pythagoras, ‖a + b‖² is the squared length of the combined arrow. Expanding it with the rules above gives ‖a‖² + 2 f(a, b) + ‖b‖².
  3. Solve for f. f(a, b) = (‖a + b‖² − ‖a‖² − ‖b‖²) / 2. For a = (3, 2) and b = (4, 0) this gives …, the same as the sum of products.
  4. Multiply it out. Writing the squared lengths as sums of squared entries, everything cancels except a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + …, so the requirements force the sum of products.

Before you continue, predict: what is the dot product of an arrow with itself?

I have made my prediction: show the reasoning

It is the squared length. For (3, 4): 3×3 + 4×4 = 25 = 5², which is why “the squared length” and “the dot product with itself” are the same thing.

07 In an LLM

  • The attention score is a dot product. In the original Transformer, each token has a query vector and a key vector, and the attention score between two tokens is the dot product of one token’s query with the other’s key (Vaswani et al., 2017, §3.2). With the made-up three-entry vectors q = (1, 0, 2) and k = (2, 1, 1), the score is …. A large positive score means the query and key lean the same way.
  • Matrix products are dot products. Computing all the scores at once is a matrix multiplication, which is why the Matrix-vector card matters.

Simplified. The vectors above are made up. Real queries and keys come from learned matrices and have tens of entries (64 in the original base model). This card only covers the score itself, not what is done with it afterwards.

Explain it back

The dot product is ‖a‖‖b‖ cos θ. The lengths are never negative, so the sign comes from the cosine. Below 90° the cosine is positive, so the arrows lean the same way. At 90° it is zero, so they are at right angles. Above 90° it is negative, so they lean opposite ways.

Teach it

60-second explanation

“Multiply matching entries and add: that is the dot product. It is also the shadow of one arrow on another, times the length of the other. Positive means they lean the same way, zero means right angles, negative means opposite. It is the way a computer asks how much two lists agree.”

Everyday analogy

A shadow at noon: the lamp is overhead and the shadow is the part of a stick that lies along the ground. Where it breaks down: a shadow has no sign; the dot product gets a minus sign when the arrow leans backwards.

Whiteboard sequence

  1. Draw two arrows from one point.
  2. Drop a perpendicular from the tip of a onto the line of b and mark the shadow.
  3. Write a·b = shadow × ‖b‖.
  4. Next to it, write a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ for the same example.
  5. Rotate b to 90° and show the shadow shrink to nothing.

Questions learners ask

Is the dot product an arrow? No. It is one number.

Why does it equal both formulas? Both come from the requirements on the card: linear in each arrow and equal to the squared length for a·a.

Does order matter? No: a·b = b·a.

Common misconception

“A big dot product means the arrows point the same way.” It may only mean the arrows are long. To compare direction alone, you divide the lengths out.
